How to play chess

Chess is played on an 8×8 board between two players, White and Black. White always moves first, then players alternate.

How the pieces move

Pawn — moves forward one square (two on its first move), captures diagonally one square forward. Reaching the last rank promotes it to a queen, rook, bishop or knight.

Knight — moves in an L-shape: two squares in one direction, then one square perpendicular. It's the only piece that can jump over others.

Bishop — moves any number of squares diagonally. Stays on one color for the whole game.

Rook — moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically.

Queen — combines the rook and bishop: any number of squares in any straight or diagonal direction.

King — moves one square in any direction. Losing the king (checkmate) ends the game.

Special moves

Castling — the king moves two squares toward a rook, and that rook jumps to the square beside the king. Only allowed if neither piece has moved, no pieces are between them, and the king isn't in or passing through check.

En passant — if an opposing pawn moves two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had only moved one square, but only immediately on the next move.

Promotion — a pawn reaching the far rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight (usually a queen).

Ending the game

Checkmate — the king is attacked and has no legal way to escape. The game ends immediately.

Stalemate — the player to move has no legal move and is not in check. The game is a draw.

Draws can also happen by threefold repetition, the 50-move rule, or insufficient material for either side to deliver checkmate.

Live TV

Bots play against each other randomly around the clock — real Stockfish, random time control, and a real clock per game. As soon as one game ends, the next one starts immediately.

Ongoing games

Honest limitation: this site runs without a server. Games keep running as long as this tab stays open — close it, and everything pauses until you reopen it. True 24/7 in the background (even with the tab closed) would need a permanently running server process, which a single HTML file can't have.

Community

Player profiles, teams and forums need accounts and a database on a server. This build of DarkChess is a single static file with no backend, so those features aren't available here.

Everything on this page (Play, Puzzles, Bots, Tournaments, Analysis, Learn, Watch) runs entirely in your browser and needs no server.
